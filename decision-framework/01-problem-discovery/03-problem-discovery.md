# 3. Problem Discovery & EB-25

## Overview

Problems first appear as symptoms, friction, or vague ideas. The discovery phase is about capturing these inputs without judgment, then aggressively filtering them through the **EB-25 (Eisenhower-Buffett 25)** methodology.

**Key Principle:** Not all problems deserve attention. We must ruthlessly prioritize to ensure executive bandwidth is spent only on high-impact issues.

## The EB-25 Methodology

The **EB-25 (Eisenhower-Buffett 25)** is our mechanism for managing the problem backlog. It combines the urgent/important filtering of the Eisenhower Matrix with Warren Buffett's 5/25 rule (forcing focus on the top tier and actively ignoring the rest).

### The Two-Filter System

```
CONTINUOUS CAPTURE → EISENHOWER FILTER → BUFFETT FILTER → TOP 5 EXECUTION
```

## Step 1: Continuous Capture

Anyone in the organization can log a symptom, friction point, or opportunity. This forms the raw backlog.

**Capture Guidelines:**
- **No judgment:** Capture everything without filtering
- **Be specific:** Describe the symptom, not the solution
- **Include context:** When did this happen? Who is affected?
- **Log immediately:** Don't wait for perfect articulation

**Capture Channels:**
- Dedicated problem backlog tool (e.g., Notion, Linear, Jira)
- Slack/Teams integration for quick logging
- Weekly problem capture sessions
- Customer feedback integration

## Step 2: Eisenhower Filtering

Before a problem is formally identified or framed, it must pass the Eisenhower filter. This filter separates problems by urgency and importance.

### The Eisenhower Matrix

| | Urgent | Not Urgent |
|---|--------|------------|
| **Important** | **DO** - Critical, time-sensitive | **SCHEDULE** - Strategic, plan for later |
| **Not Important** | **DELEGATE** - Interrupts but low value | **DELETE** - Time-wasters, eliminate |

**Definitions:**
- **Urgent:** Requires immediate attention (time-sensitive, deadline-driven)
- **Important:** Contributes to long-term goals and values (strategic)

### Application Rules

| Eisenhower Classification | Action | EB-25 Destination |
| ------------------------ | ------ | ----------------- |
| **Urgent + Important** | DO immediately | Top 5 (if capacity) or Next 20 |
| **Not Urgent + Important** | Schedule for framing | Next 20 |
| **Urgent + Not Important** | Delegate or minimize | DELETE (remove from backlog) |
| **Not Urgent + Not Important** | Delete/decline | DELETE (remove from backlog) |

**Key Principle:** Only **Important** problems (whether Urgent or Not) enter the EB-25. Not Important problems are eliminated entirely.

### Eisenhower Filtering Examples

**Example 1: Server Outage**
- **Symptom:** Production server is down, customers cannot access service
- **Eisenhower:** Urgent + Important
- **Action:** DO immediately
- **EB-25:** Top 5 (if capacity allows)

**Example 2: Improve Documentation**
- **Symptom:** New team members struggle to onboard due to poor documentation
- **Eisenhower:** Not Urgent + Important
- **Action:** Schedule for framing
- **EB-25:** Next 20

**Example 3: Update Office Decor**
- **Symptom:** Office furniture looks dated
- **Eisenhower:** Not Urgent + Not Important
- **Action:** Delete
- **EB-25:** DELETE (removed from backlog)

## Step 3: Buffett's 5/25 Rule

Warren Buffett's 5/25 rule provides the capacity constraint. The problem backlog is strictly limited to 25 active items.

### The Three Tiers

**The Top 5 (Active):**
- These are the problems currently being framed and actively assigned RFCs
- Executive bandwidth is focused exclusively on these 5 problems
- When a Top 5 problem is resolved, it is removed from the backlog
- When bandwidth opens, a problem from the Next 20 is promoted

**The Next 20 (Waiting):**
- These are important problems waiting for executive bandwidth
- They are scheduled and prioritized, but not actively worked on
- No RFCs are drafted for Next 20 problems
- When promoted to Top 5, they proceed to Problem Identification

**The Avoid-at-All-Costs List (Prohibited):**
- Anything that didn't make the Top 25 must receive **zero** attention
- This is not a backlog—it's an active prohibition
- These problems are ignored until the Top 5 are resolved
- If a problem truly matters, it will resurface through the Eisenhower filter

### The Buffett Philosophy

Warren Buffett's advice to his pilot:
1. Write down your top 25 priorities
2. Circle the top 5
3. The other 20 become your "Avoid-at-All-Costs" list
4. Give zero attention to the Avoid list until the top 5 are complete

**Why This Works:**
- Creates scarcity mindset
- Forces ruthless prioritization
- Prevents attention dilution
- Ensures focus on what truly matters

### EB-25 Capacity Management

**Adding a New Problem:**
- If Top 5 has capacity (< 5 items): Add to Top 5
- If Top 5 is full: Add to Next 20 (if < 20 items)
- If Next 20 is full: Must remove or demote an existing item

**Promoting from Next 20:**
- When a Top 5 problem is resolved, promote the highest-priority Next 20 item
- Re-evaluate Next 20 priorities quarterly
- Demote Top 5 items that lose importance

**Removing from Backlog:**
- Problems that lose importance are removed entirely
- Resolved problems are archived (not kept in active backlog)
- Problems that were misclassified are deleted

## Step 4: Top 5 Execution

Only problems in the Top 5 proceed to the **Problem Identification** phase.

**Top 5 Workflow:**
1. **Problem Identification:** Apply 5 Whys, construct Problem Statement
2. **RFC Drafting:** Propose solution architecture and approach
3. **RFC Review:** Open for peer review and critique
4. **RFC Finalization:** Mark as ready for executive decision
5. **Handoff to Decision Tree:** Enter Decision Tree State Gathering phase

**Top 5 Capacity:**
- Maximum 5 problems actively being worked on at any time
- Each problem in Top 5 should have an assigned owner
- Regular progress reviews to ensure Top 5 is moving forward

## EB-25 Best Practices

### Practice 1: Weekly Eisenhower Review

Conduct a weekly review of the raw backlog to apply Eisenhower filtering.

**Why:**
- Prevents backlog bloat
- Ensures timely classification
- Maintains backlog health
- Identifies emerging important problems

### Practice 2: Quarterly Buffett Review

Conduct a quarterly review of the EB-25 tiers to reassess priorities.

**Why:**
- Priorities change over time
- New problems emerge
- Old problems may lose importance
- Ensures EB-25 reflects current strategic focus

### Practice 3: Zero Attention to Avoid List

Actively ignore problems on the Avoid-at-All-Costs list.

**Why:**
- Prevents attention dilution
- Forces focus on Top 5
- Creates scarcity mindset
- Validates prioritization decisions

### Practice 4: Promote Based on Impact

When promoting from Next 20 to Top 5, prioritize based on impact, not urgency.

**Why:**
- Urgency alone is insufficient
- Impact-driven prioritization aligns with strategy
- Prevents constant fire-fighting
- Ensures strategic problems get attention

## Common EB-25 Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Backlog Bloat

**Symptom:** The raw backlog grows without bound, making Eisenhower filtering overwhelming.

**Solution:** Conduct weekly Eisenhower reviews. Delete or delegate Not Important problems immediately.

### Pitfall 2: Avoid List Violation

**Symptom:** Giving attention to problems on the Avoid-at-All-Costs list.

**Solution:** Treat the Avoid list as a prohibition, not a backlog. If a problem truly matters, it will resurface through Eisenhower filtering.

### Pitfall 3: Top 5 Overload

**Symptom:** Trying to work on more than 5 problems simultaneously.

**Solution:** Strictly enforce the Top 5 limit. If a new problem is critical, demote an existing Top 5 item to Next 20.

### Pitfall 4: Urgency-Driven Prioritization

**Symptom:** Promoting problems to Top 5 based solely on urgency, not importance.

**Solution:** Use Eisenhower filtering correctly. Only Important problems enter EB-25, regardless of urgency.

### Pitfall 5: Static Priorities

**Symptom:** Never re-evaluating EB-25 priorities, leading to stale backlog.

**Solution:** Conduct quarterly Buffett reviews to reassess priorities and adjust tiers.

## EB-25 Examples

### Example 1: Startup Scaling Problem

**Raw Capture:** "We're having trouble hiring fast enough"

**Eisenhower Filter:**
- Urgent: Yes (hiring is time-sensitive)
- Important: Yes (critical for growth)
- Classification: Urgent + Important
- Action: DO immediately
- EB-25: Top 5

**Buffett Filter:**
- Current Top 5 capacity: 4/5
- Action: Add to Top 5
- Proceed to Problem Identification

### Example 2: Technical Debt Problem

**Raw Capture:** "Our codebase has accumulated significant technical debt"

**Eisenhower Filter:**
- Urgent: No (not immediately time-sensitive)
- Important: Yes (affects long-term maintainability)
- Classification: Not Urgent + Important
- Action: Schedule for framing
- EB-25: Next 20

**Buffett Filter:**
- Current Next 20 capacity: 18/20
- Action: Add to Next 20
- Wait for promotion to Top 5

### Example 3: Low-Priority Feature Request

**Raw Capture:** "Users want dark mode in the admin panel"

**Eisenhower Filter:**
- Urgent: No
- Important: No (nice-to-have, not strategic)
- Classification: Not Urgent + Not Important
- Action: Delete
- EB-25: DELETE (removed from backlog)

**Buffett Filter:**
- Action: Do not add to EB-25
- Remove from raw backlog

## Moving to Identification

When bandwidth opens up in the Top 5, an item from the Next 20 is promoted. Only problems in the Top 5 proceed to the **Problem Identification** phase.

**Promotion Criteria:**
- Highest strategic impact
- Alignment with current quarterly goals
- Resource availability
- Dependencies on other problems
