# 4. Problem Identification

## Overview

Once a problem enters the Top 5 of the EB-25, it must be formally identified. The goal of this phase is to write a precise, solution-agnostic **Problem Statement**.

## Stripping Solution Bias

The most common mistake in problem identification is embedding the solution into the problem.

* **Bad:** "We need a new CRM." (This is a solution masquerading as a problem).
* **Good:** "Our sales team spends 40% of their time manually entering lead data across three disconnected tools, leading to a 15% drop in follow-ups."

## The 5 Whys

To reach the root cause, apply the 5 Whys technique. When presented with a symptom, ask "Why?" five times until the fundamental systemic issue is uncovered.

## Constructing the Problem Statement

A well-formed Problem Statement must include:

1. **Context:** What is the current situation?
2. **Friction:** What is the specific pain point or gap?
3. **Impact:** What is the measurable cost of this friction (time, money, morale, risk)?
4. **Constraint:** What are the non-negotiable boundaries for a solution?

### Success Criteria

Before moving to RFC, define what a successful resolution looks like. If we solve this problem, what metric moves, and by how much?
