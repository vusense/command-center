# Board Charter

## Vusense Limited (the "Company")

---



## Part 1 - Interpretation

In this charter:

* **Act** means the Companies Act 1993.
* **Board** means the board of directors of the company.
* **Business** means the business of the company.
* **CEO** means the chief executive officer of the company.
* **Chair** means the chair of the board.
* **Company** means Vusense Limited.
* **Company goals** means the goals of the company as set out in Part 2.
* **CTO** means the chief technology officer of the company.
* **Deputy chair** means the deputy chair of the board.
* **EB-25 Protocol** means the Company's proprietary operational gating matrix combining the Eisenhower Matrix and the Buffett 25/5 Priority Framework.
* **Executive Director** means a member of the Board who also holds full-time executive office within the Company (specifically the CEO and the CTO).
* **Management** means the management personnel of the company.
* **Management limitations** means the limitations on the actions of management as set out in Part 4.
* **Material Strategic Decision** means any decision, initiative, or corporate action that has cleared the threshold of the EB-25 Protocol.
* **Members** means members of the board.
* **Secretary** means the board secretary or the person normally exercising the functions of a board secretary.
* **Shareholders** means the shareholders of the company.

## Part 2 - Company Goals

### General

* **2.1** The purpose of the company is to establish a foundational mechanism for trust in visual media.
* **2.2** The capital and resources of the company will be allocated to those assets and activities which will enable it to achieve the company goals in a manner best serving the interests of the shareholders as a whole.

## Part 3 - Board Governance Process

### Role of the Board

* **3.1** The role of the board is to effectively represent, and promote the interests of, the company with a view to adding long-term value to the company's shares. Having regard to its role the board will direct, and supervise the management of, the business and affairs of the company including, in particular:

  * ensuring that the company goals are clearly established, and strategies are in place for achieving them.
  * establishing policies for strengthening the performance of the company including ensuring that management is proactively seeking to build the business through innovation, initiative, technology, new products and the development of its business capital.
  * monitoring the performance of management.
  * appointing the CEO and CTO, setting the terms of their employment contracts and, where necessary, terminating their employment with the company.
  * deciding on whatever steps are necessary to protect the company's financial position and the ability to meet its debts and other obligations when they fall due, and ensuring that such steps are taken.
  * ensuring the company's financial statements are true and fair and otherwise conform with law.
  * ensuring the company adheres to high standards of ethics and corporate behaviour.
  * ensuring the company has appropriate risk management/regulatory compliance policies in place.
* **3.2** In the normal course of events, day-to-day management of the company will be in the hands of management.
* **3.3** The board will satisfy itself that the company is achieving the company goals.

### Board Relationship with Shareholders

* **3.4** The board will use its best endeavours to familiarise itself with issues of concern to shareholders.
* **3.5** The board will regularly evaluate economic, political, social and legal issues and any other relevant external matters that may influence or affect the development of the business or the interests of shareholders and, if thought appropriate, will take outside expert advice on these matters.

### Board Relationship with Other Stakeholders

* **3.6** The board will use its best endeavours to familiarise itself with issues of concern to all relevant stakeholders.
* **3.7** The board recognises that the company's long-term survival and prosperity are closely intertwined with the environments and markets within which it operates and the extent to which the company is seen as a responsible corporate citizen.

### Board Procedures

* **3.8** The conduct of members will be consistent with their duties and responsibilities to the company and, indirectly, to shareholders.
* **3.9** The board will be disciplined in carrying out its role, with the emphasis on strategic issues and policy. Members will always act within any limitations imposed by the board on its activities.
* **3.10** Members will use their best endeavours to attend board meetings and to prepare thoroughly. Members are expected to participate fully, frankly and constructively in board discussions and other activities and to bring the benefit of their particular knowledge, skills and abilities to the board table. Members unable to attend a meeting will advise the chair at the earliest date possible and confirm in writing to the secretary.
* **3.11** Board discussions will be open and constructive, recognising that genuinely-held differences of opinion can, in such circumstances, bring greater clarity and lead to better decisions.
* **3.12** The chair will seek a consensus in the board. For routine operational or business-as-usual matters, the chair may call for a vote. However, any matter designated as a Material Strategic Decision via the EB-25 Protocol strictly requires a unanimous affirmative vote (100% consensus) of all Executive Directors at the Board level.
* **3.13** In the event of an absolute 50/50 voting deadlock on a Material Strategic Decision or an item requiring unanimous consent, the following mandatory escalation sequence is triggered:

  * **The STOP Protocol:** The Board shall enter a mandatory 72-hour cooling-off period to isolate the precise technical or commercial problem statement, completely stripped of execution or personal bias.
  * **Binding Technical Arbitration:** If the deadlock persists and involves technical architecture, source code deployment, engineering infrastructure, cryptographic protocols, or intellectual property security, the matter shall be referred immediately to a mutually agreed-upon Independent Technical Advisor acting as an expert. Their objective determination shall be final and legally binding on both parties.
  * **Maintenance of Status Quo:** If the deadlock persists and involves an unbudgeted commercial strategy, equity structuring, or corporate pivot, the status quo shall prevail. Neither Executive Director may execute the disputed action without mutual founder alignment.
  * **Exclusion of Shareholder Override:** All Material Strategic Decisions and unanimous board requirements are explicitly excluded from being resolved via an Ordinary Resolution of shareholders using a simple shareholding majority (>50%). The shareholding equity layer (including the CEO's 66% voting allocation) is legally restricted from breaking a Board deadlock on these matters.
* **3.14** All discussions and their record will remain confidential unless there is a specific direction from the board to the contrary, or disclosure is required by law. Subject to legal or regulatory requirements the board will decide the manner and timing of the publication of its decisions.
* **3.15** Executive members attend board meetings to discharge their board responsibilities. At board meetings, board responsibilities supersede all executive responsibilities.
* **3.16** The board has sole authority over its agenda and exercises this through the chair. Any member may, through the chair, request the addition of an item to the agenda. The agenda will be set by the chair in consultation with the CEO, CTO, and the secretary.
* **3.17** The board will normally hold meetings in each month of the year except January and will hold additional meetings as the occasion requires. At each normal meeting the company's interests register will be updated as necessary and the board will consider:

  * an operational report from the CEO.
  * a technical and product report from the CTO.
  * a report from the CFO.
  * reports on their activities from the company's individual business units.
  * specific proposals for capital expenditure and acquisitions.
  * major issues and opportunities for the company.
* **3.18** In addition the board will, at intervals of not more than one year:

  * review the company goals.
  * review the strategies and operating plans for achieving the company goals.
  * approve the annual budget.
  * approve the annual and half-yearly financial statements, reports to shareholders and public announcements.
  * approve the annual report.
  * consider and, if appropriate, declare or recommend the payment of dividends.
  * review the board composition, structure and succession.
  * review the company's audit requirements.
  * review the performance and composition of, and necessity for board committees.
  * undertake board and individual member evaluations.
  * review members' remuneration.
  * review the CEO's and CTO's performance and remuneration.
  * review remuneration policies and practices in general including superannuation and incentive schemes for management.
  * review risk assessment policies and controls including insurance covers and compliance with legal and regulatory requirements.
  * review the company's code of conduct and ethical standards.
  * review shareholder, customer and supplier relations.
  * review donations and sponsorships.
  * settle the following year's board work plan.
* **3.19** Members are entitled to have access, at all reasonable times, to all relevant company information and to management. Members are expected to strictly observe the provisions of the Act applicable to the use and confidentiality of company information.
* **3.20** In making policy, the board will not reach specific decisions unless it has considered the more general principles upon which they are founded, and in reaching other specific decisions the board will consider the policies against which the decisions are made.

### Chair and Deputy Chair

* **3.21** Each year the board will appoint from among its members a chair and deputy chair. Until such time as independent, non-executive directors are appointed, the positions of Chair and Deputy Chair shall rotate annually between the two founding Executive Directors upon mutual agreement. Holding the office of Chair carries no additional casting, veto, or tie-breaking vote at the Board level.
* **3.22** The deputy chair will deputise for the chair in their absence or at their request.
* **3.23** The chair is responsible for:

  * representing the board to shareholders.
  * ensuring the integrity and effectiveness of the governance process of the board as set out in Part 3.
  * maintaining regular dialogue with executive leadership over all operational matters and consulting with the remainder of the board promptly over any matter that gives cause for major concern.
* **3.24** The chair will act as facilitator at meetings of the board to ensure that no member dominates discussion, that appropriate discussion takes place and that relevant opinion among members is forthcoming. The chair will ensure that discussions result in logical and understandable outcomes.

### Board Committees

* **3.25** Board committees will be formed only when it is efficient or necessary to facilitate efficient decision-making. Board committees will observe the same rules of conduct and procedure as the board unless the board determines otherwise. Board committees will only speak or act for the board when so authorised. The authority conferred on a board committee will not derogate from the authority delegated to the executive officers.
* **3.26** The board shall maintain standing audit, remuneration, and nomination committees as the corporate scale requires. Until institutional scaling is reached, the functions of these committees will be executed directly by the full Board, ensuring that structural alterations to technical assets or capitalization remain bound to unanimous board consensus.

### Board Composition 

* **3.27** The composition of the board will reflect the duties and responsibilities it is to discharge and perform as representative of the interests of shareholders, and in setting the company's strategy and seeing that it is implemented.
* **3.28** Generally, the qualifications for board membership are the ability and intelligence to make sensible business decisions and recommendations, an entrepreneurial talent for contributing to the creation of shareholder value, the ability to see the wider picture, the ability to ask the hard questions, experience in the industry sector, high ethical standards, sound practical sense, and a total commitment to furthering the interests of shareholders and the achievement of the company goals.
* **3.29** The initial composition of the Board is restricted to the two founding Executive Directors, ensuring absolute symmetric authority during the foundational deep-tech development phase. As the company expands, the size of the board will scale to ensure a broader range of knowledge, views, and experience.

### Induction of New Members

* **3.30** Genuine potential members are encouraged to carry out due diligence on the company before accepting an appointment to the board. On their first appointment, new members will have the benefit of an induction programme aimed at deepening their understanding of the company and the business and the environment and markets in which the company operates. As part of the programme, members will receive a folder of essential board and company information and will meet key management.
* **3.31** Members are expected to keep themselves abreast of changes and trends in the business and in the company's environment and markets and to keep abreast of changes and trends in the economic, political, social and legal climate generally.

### Members' Remuneration

* **3.32** The board will determine the level of remuneration paid to members within any limitations imposed by shareholders. Executive members will receive no separate director fees but will be paid as employees of the company in accordance with their contracts of employment with the company.

### Shareholdings by Members in the Company

* **3.33** Members are encouraged to hold shares in the company, recognising that this has the capacity to increase the focus of members on company performance and share value. When buying or selling shares members must strictly observe the provisions of the company's constitution, the company's own internal rules, the Act, and all relevant legislative or regulatory procedures.

### Provision of Business or Professional Services by Members

* **3.34** Because a conflict of interest (actual or perceived) may be created, members should not, generally, provide business or professional services of an ongoing nature to the company. Notwithstanding this rule, the company is at liberty to, for the purpose of a special assignment, engage the services of any member having special expertise, so long as the terms of engagement are competitive, clearly recorded, and all legal requirements for disclosure of the engagement are properly observed.

### Other Board Appointments

* **3.35** Any member is, while holding office, at liberty to accept other board appointments so long as the appointment is not in conflict with the business and does not detrimentally affect their performance as a member. All other appointments must first be discussed with the Chair before being accepted.

### Independent Professional Advice

* **3.36** Any member is entitled to obtain independent professional advice relating to the affairs of the company or to their other responsibilities as a member. If a member considers such advice is necessary the member shall first discuss it with the chair and, having done so, shall be free to proceed. Subject to the prior approval of the chair, the cost of the advice will be reimbursed by the company.

### Board and Member Evaluations

* **3.37** The board will, each year, critically evaluate its own performance, and its own processes and procedures to ensure that they are not unduly complex and are designed to assist the board in effectively fulfilling its role. Individual member and chair evaluations shall be executed annually in writing through an objective review process managed by the board.

### Indemnities and Insurance

* **3.38** Subject to the company's constitution the company will provide members with, and will pay the premiums for, indemnity and insurance cover while acting in their capacities as members, to the fullest extent permitted by the Act.

### The Company Secretary

* **3.39** The appointment of the company secretary is made on the recommendation of the CEO and must be approved by the board. The company secretary is responsible for ensuring that board procedures are followed, that applicable rules and regulations are complied with, and for all matters associated with the efficient operation of the board. All members have equal access to the advice and services of the company secretary.

## Part 4 - Board/Management Relationship

### Position of CEO and CTO

* **4.1** The board links the company's governance and management functions through its executive officers. Pursuant to Section 130 of the Act, executive management is formally split into two autonomous domains:

  * **Commercial Management:** Delegated directly to the CEO, encompassing exclusive day-to-day authority over commercial operations, sales, financial management, corporate marketing, and general operational administration.
  * **Technical Management:** Delegated directly to the CTO, encompassing exclusive day-to-day authority over technical architecture, product engineering, source code deployment, information security, infrastructure configurations, and intellectual property development.
* **4.2** All board authority conferred on management within these domains is delegated through the CEO and CTO respectively, so that the authority and accountability of management is considered the authority and accountability of the specific domain lead so far as the board is concerned. Only decisions of the board acting as a body are binding on the executive officers.

### Accountability of Executive Leadership to the Board

* **4.3** The CEO and CTO are symmetrically accountable to the board for the achievement of the company goals within their respective domains, and for the strict observance of the management limitations. At each of its normal monthly meetings the board should expect to receive from or through management operational, technical, and other reports and such assurances as the board considers necessary to confirm that the management limitations are being observed.

### Management Limitations

* **4.4** The executive officers are expected to act within all specific authorities delegated to them by the board. Management is explicitly restricted from causing or permitting any practice, activity, or decision that is contrary to commonly accepted good business practice or professional ethics.
* **4.5** In financing the company and allocating its resources, management is expected to adhere strictly to the company goals and avoid any action likely to result in the company becoming financially embarrassed. Company assets must be adequately maintained, protected, and operated with a comprehensive system of internal controls acceptable to the company's external auditors.
* **4.6 Financial Delegations & Caps:** To preserve corporate velocity, either the CEO or the CTO is granted standing authority to execute unbudgeted expenditures up to **NZD $25,000 per transaction** strictly within their respective operational domain (Commercial or Technical), provided that:

  * The expenditure does not cause total monthly unbudgeted cash outflow to exceed the board-approved runway budget by more than 10%.
  * The transaction does not create a recurring financial obligation exceeding 12 months.
* **4.7 Prior Unanimous Board Approval** is strictly required for any single unbudgeted transaction exceeding **NZD $5,000**, or any unbudgeted full-time personnel hiring commitment carrying a permanent annual salary obligation. Departmental hiring, contracting, and termination within a previously board-approved budget remain under the sole discretion of the respective domain lead.
* **4.8** Management is expected to not cause or permit anyone to substitute their own risk preferences for those of the shareholders as a whole. Core strategic pivots, operational workflow modifications, and problem discovery methodologies (including the 4X Discovery Engine) shall be managed dynamically within the team workspace, but cannot bypass or override the statutory limitations and unanimity parameters established by this Board Charter.
