# 2. Framework Philosophy

## Core Principles

### 1. Problem Before Solution

Never propose a solution without first understanding the problem. Jumping to solution selection without problem discovery leads to solving the wrong problem or creating solutions in search of problems.

**Why This Matters:**
- Teams often fall in love with solutions and go looking for problems
- Solution-first thinking wastes executive time on non-issues
- Well-framed problems make solution selection straightforward
- Prevents "hammer looking for a nail" syndrome

### 2. Aggressive Prioritization

Not all problems deserve attention. The EB-25 methodology ensures we focus only on problems that matter by combining Eisenhower Matrix urgency/importance filtering with Buffett's 5/25 rule.

**Why This Matters:**
- Early-stage startups have limited executive bandwidth
- Attention is the scarcest resource
- Solving low-impact problems distracts from existential threats
- Forces ruthless focus on what truly matters

### 3. Solution Agnosticism

Problem identification must strip away all solution bias. A well-formed Problem Statement describes what is wrong, not how to fix it.

**Why This Matters:**
- Prevents premature solution lock-in
- Enables creative, non-obvious solutions
- Reduces cofounder friction over implementation details
- Allows multiple competing RFCs for the same problem

### 4. Asynchronous Collaboration

RFC methodology decouples the person from the idea and enables thoughtful, non-personal critique through asynchronous review.

**Why This Matters:**
- Reduces meeting overhead and synchronous friction
- Allows deeper, more considered feedback
- Creates audit trail of all objections and considerations
- Prevents personality-driven decision-making

### 5. Root Cause Analysis

Symptoms are not problems. The 5 Whys technique ensures we address fundamental systemic issues rather than surface-level manifestations.

**Why This Matters:**
- Solving symptoms leads to recurring problems
- Root cause analysis prevents whack-a-mole management
- Systemic solutions have compounding benefits
- Reduces long-term technical and operational debt

## The "Meta" Approach

### Traditional Problem-Solving Problem

Most organizations skip problem discovery entirely and jump straight to solution discussion. This creates problems:

- **Solution-First Thinking:** Teams propose solutions before understanding the problem
- **Priority Dilution:** All problems get equal attention regardless of impact
- **Alignment Friction:** Cofounders argue over solutions without agreeing on the problem
- **Wasted Executive Time:** Low-impact problems consume high-value bandwidth

### Framework Solution

Our framework treats problem discovery as a prerequisite to decision-making:

```
PROBLEM DISCOVERY (EB-25) → PROBLEM IDENTIFICATION → RFC METHODOLOGY → HANDOFF TO DECISION TREE
```

This ensures:
- Only high-impact problems reach executive attention
- Problems are well-understood before solutions are discussed
- Multiple solution options can be evaluated objectively
- Decision Tree processes work on well-framed inputs

### The Two-Layer Model

```
Layer 1: Problem Framing Meta-Framework (This Document)
    ↓ Discovers, identifies, and prioritizes problems
Layer 2: Decision Tree Meta-Framework
    ↓ Selects appropriate decision framework based on context
Layer 3: Decision Frameworks (STOP, ASOFF, ICE, etc.)
    ↓ Provides structured decision methodology
Layer 4: Decision Templates (Specific documentation)
    ↓ Captures decision process and outcome
```

## Problem Dimensions

Our meta-framework categorizes problems along three key dimensions:

### Dimension 1: Urgency

How time-sensitive is this problem?

- **Urgent:** Requires immediate attention (time-sensitive, deadline-driven)
- **Not Urgent:** Can be addressed strategically (no immediate deadline)

**Philosophy:** Urgency alone is insufficient justification. Urgent but unimportant problems should be delegated or deleted.

### Dimension 2: Importance

How much does this problem matter to long-term goals and values?

- **Important:** Contributes to strategic objectives, mission, or survival
- **Not Important:** Low impact on long-term outcomes

**Philosophy:** Importance without urgency is the strategic sweet spot. These problems should be scheduled and batched.

### Dimension 3: Focus Tier

Where does this problem fall in the EB-25 prioritization?

- **Top 5:** Currently being framed and actively assigned RFCs
- **Next 20:** Important problems waiting for executive bandwidth
- **Avoid-at-All-Costs:** Problems receiving zero attention until Top 5 are resolved

**Philosophy:** The Avoid-at-All-Costs list is not a backlog—it's a prohibition. These problems must be actively ignored.

## Framework Design Principles

### Principle 1: Strict Capacity Limits

The EB-25 methodology enforces a hard limit of 25 active problems. No exceptions.

**Why:**
- Forces ruthless prioritization
- Prevents backlog bloat
- Creates scarcity mindset
- Ensures focus on what matters

### Principle 2: Solution-Agnostic Documentation

Problem Statements must never contain solution language or implementation details.

**Why:**
- Enables creative problem-solving
- Reduces solution lock-in bias
- Allows competing RFCs
- Separates "what" from "how"

### Principle 3: Asynchronous Review

RFCs must go through a defined review period before finalization.

**Why:**
- Reduces meeting overhead
- Enables deeper consideration
- Creates audit trail
- Decouples person from idea

### Principle 4: Root Cause Validation

Problem identification requires 5 Whys analysis to reach systemic issues.

**Why:**
- Prevents symptom-solving
- Addresses fundamental issues
- Reduces recurring problems
- Creates systemic improvements

### Principle 5: Handoff Standards

Only problems with completed Problem Statements and finalized RFCs may enter the Decision Tree.

**Why:**
- Ensures decision quality
- Streamlines decision process
- Reduces alignment friction
- Maintains framework integrity

## The EB-25 Philosophy

### Eisenhower Matrix Integration

The Eisenhower Matrix provides the urgency/importance filtering:

| | Urgent | Not Urgent |
|---|--------|------------|
| **Important** | **DO** - Proceed to Top 5 | **SCHEDULE** - Proceed to Next 20 |
| **Not Important** | **DELEGATE** - Remove from backlog | **DELETE** - Remove from backlog |

**Philosophy:** Only Important problems (whether Urgent or Not) enter the EB-25. Not Important problems are eliminated entirely.

### Buffett's 5/25 Rule Integration

Warren Buffett's 5/25 rule provides the capacity constraint:

1. Write down your top 25 priorities
2. Circle the top 5
3. Avoid the other 20 at all costs until the top 5 are complete

**Philosophy:** The "Avoid-at-All-Costs" list is not a backlog—it's an active prohibition. These problems receive zero attention.

### The Combined EB-25 Approach

```
EISENHOWER FILTER (Urgency + Importance)
    ↓
BUFFETT FILTER (Top 5 + Next 20 + Avoid)
    ↓
FOCUSED EXECUTION (Only Top 5 active)
```

**Philosophy:** EB-25 combines the filtering power of Eisenhower with the focus discipline of Buffett to ensure executive attention is spent only on problems that truly matter.

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

### Anti-Pattern 1: Solution-First Problem Statement

**Description:** Writing "We need a new CRM" as a problem statement.

**Solution:** Strip to the actual problem: "Our sales team spends 40% of their time manually entering lead data across three disconnected tools."

### Anti-Pattern 2: Backlog Bloat

**Description:** Allowing the problem backlog to grow beyond 25 items.

**Solution:** Strictly enforce EB-25 limits. If a new problem enters, an existing one must be removed or demoted.

### Anti-Pattern 3: Symptom Solving

**Description:** Addressing surface-level issues without root cause analysis.

**Solution:** Apply 5 Whys technique to reach fundamental systemic issues before problem identification.

### Anti-Pattern 4: RFC Skipping

**Description:** Moving directly from Problem Statement to Decision Tree without RFC.

**Solution:** RFC is mandatory for all problems entering the Decision Tree. No exceptions.

### Anti-Pattern 5: Avoid List Violation

**Description:** Giving attention to problems on the Avoid-at-All-Costs list.

**Solution:** Actively ignore Avoid list problems. If they become truly important, they'll naturally resurface through the Eisenhower filter.

## Success Metrics

We measure problem framing success along three dimensions:

### 1. Problem Quality
- Are Problem Statements solution-agnostic?
- Do Problem Statements include clear success criteria?
- Is root cause analysis thorough (5 Whys applied)?
- Are problems well-framed before solutions are discussed?

### 2. Prioritization Effectiveness
- Is the EB-25 backlog maintained at 25 items or fewer?
- Are Top 5 problems actually being worked on?
- Is the Avoid-at-All-Costs list being actively ignored?
- Does executive time align with Top 5 priorities?

### 3. Decision Readiness
- Do RFCs provide clear solution options?
- Are trade-offs and risks well-documented?
- Do Problem Statements and RFCs streamline Decision Tree process?
- Is alignment on problem statement achieved before decision-making?

## Continuous Evolution

This meta-framework is not static. It evolves through:

1. **Quarterly Reviews:** Assess EB-25 backlog health and prioritization effectiveness
2. **Retrospection Insights:** Learn from Decision Tree retrospection about problem framing quality
3. **Company Scaling:** Adjust as company grows and problem complexity increases
4. **Cofounder Feedback:** Incorporate experience and suggestions on framework usability

**Philosophy:** The framework serves the company, not the other way around. Adapt when needed, but change deliberately.
